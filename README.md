A native Swift macOS space trading game presented by XYZZY as the Right-On Commander version. Its historical reference is the BBC Micro 6502 Second Processor Executive version of Elite. It uses keyboard flight controls, white wireframe ships by default, a colour cockpit scanner, procedural galaxies, and the original-style bitmap font requested for this remake. Normal games begin as Commander Jameson with a standard Cobra Mk III and 100 credits. Entering XYZZY switches to Commander Clarke with maximum hardware; coloured wireframes and shaded panels are optional display modes.

## Build and run

Requires macOS 14 or newer and Swift 6.0 or newer (Xcode or the Command Line Tools).

```sh
git clone git@github.com:stephenlclarke/elite.git
cd elite
make run
```

The Run action builds and opens `dist/Elite.app`. You can also open that app from Finder after building it. The project has no external game assets or package dependencies. It starts with an automatic XYZZY demo, then a rotating Right-On Commander title screen. Press Space to enter the commander screen and 0 to launch.

Held flight controls are cleared when the game loses focus. Number keys on the main keyboard or numeric keypad select the same screens.

### Trackpad flight

Place the pointer over the game before touching the trackpad. Touch gently; no click or repeated swipe is needed. Controls repeat on every game update while your fingers remain on the pad.

| Fingers | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Two | Steer without changing speed: up/down pitches, left/right rolls |
| One | Slow down along the current heading, stopping at zero speed |
| Three | Accelerate along the current heading, stopping at maximum speed |
| None | Centre the yoke, stop pitch/roll and cruise at the current speed |

Two-finger steering uses the midpoint of your fingers relative to the centre of the physical trackpad. Hold off centre to keep turning; return to the centre to stop turning and cruise. A small central dead zone prevents drift. Changing finger count switches modes immediately, so dropping from three fingers to two holds the speed you have reached. One- and three-finger speed changes keep the yoke centred.

Trackpad flight operates in cockpit views with manual control. Pause, help, docking, the docking computer, other screens, lost focus and cancelled gestures clear its input. Keyboard flight remains available. macOS Mission Control and three-finger dragging may intercept touches; assign system gestures to four fingers or disable three-finger dragging if three-finger acceleration does not register. Physical three-finger delivery depends on those system settings.

### Spin Hold prototype

Press V, or choose **Game → Toggle Spin Hold**, to enter the explicit spin mode. Draw a deliberate one-finger circle around the physical trackpad's centre to select clockwise or anticlockwise roll. Every recognised circle commands a gentle 12 degrees/second, independently of how quickly you draw it. CW and CCW are defined from the pilot's forward cockpit view. Pure roll preserves the forward heading.

| Fingers, in Spin Hold | Action |
| --- | --- |
| One, held still | Gradually reduce roll-speed magnitude to zero, without reversing |
| Two | Hold roll speed; move together to aim using pitch and yaw |
| Three | Gradually increase roll-speed magnitude to the cap |
| None | Keep the set spin speed, allowing your hand to lift and reposition |

The HUD shows Spin Hold state, CW/CCW direction, an arrow and actual degrees/second. It shows the target separately while the ship is changing rate, plus circle-recognition and speed-adjustment feedback. Press B, or choose **Game → Stop Spin**, to command a smooth stop and return to normal controls. V also exits smoothly. An opposite circle commands the other direction through a gradual stop and reversal. Help, pause, other screens, focus loss, cancelled gestures, docking and the docking computer cancel spin immediately.

Keyboard alternatives are [ for anticlockwise, ] for clockwise, and held - / = or + to slow / speed up. In Spin Hold, Left/Right provide yaw and X/S provide pitch. Space and slash still change forward cruising speed. Normal-flight trackpad gestures retain the separate forward-speed mapping above; Spin Hold fingers control roll speed instead.

Prototype defaults are 12 degrees/second initially, 6 degrees/second of rate adjustment per second, a 60 degrees/second cap and a 24 degrees/second-per-second response limit. Contact counts must remain stable for 120 ms before changing rate. Circles need at least 300 degrees of consistent motion, 16 samples and 85% direction consistency, at a normalized radius of 0.12-0.46 around the pad centre; jumps over 45 degrees between samples are rejected. Two-finger aiming starts from a fresh reference whenever the count changes, with a 2% dead zone and full deflection after 25% of pad travel. These are tunable prototype values, not findings from player testing. Native touch hardware and macOS gesture interaction still require an on-device check; no global gesture settings are changed by the game.

Commander saves, including secret options, live at `~/.xyzzy/elite/commander.json`. Saving is atomic; loading validates the entire commander before replacing actor state.

## Original BBC naval missions

The original two BBC Second Processor missions are enabled, including the Executive build. They begin automatically when you dock and qualify; there is no acceptance menu or deadline. An incoming message appears for two seconds before the briefing. Space, Return or Escape continues through the pages. K opens the relevant key guide; Command-P pauses the briefing or game.

The Constrictor Hunt starts in galaxy 1 or 2 at 256 combat points. Read system data with 6 for the original clue trail; clue visits are optional. The stolen ship appears at Orarra in galaxy 2. It has 252 energy, ECM, four missiles and bomb immunity. Ordinary lasers cannot damage it; a military laser inflicts five damage per hit, a quarter of its normal 23 points rounded down. Returning to a station after its removal awards 5,000 credits and 256 combat points once. The Executive source also treats the target leaving the local space bubble as mission completion; this historical quirk is retained.

Thargoid Plans starts on docking in galaxy 3 after the Hunt is finished and your tally reaches 1,280. Report to Ceerdi, collect the encoded plans, then dock at Birera. Visiting Birera before collection does nothing. The plans occupy no cargo space and survive commander save/load. Carrying them enables the original 56-in-256 Thargoid ambush predicate at eligible spawning checks, with a mothership and Thargon companion. Motherships can release six additional drones when damaged; surviving drones stop fighting after the last mothership is destroyed. Delivery replaces a standard energy unit with the naval unit and adds 256 combat points once, with no cash payment. Energy recharge follows the original 1:2:3 ratio for an unupgraded, standard and naval unit. The naval unit is a mission reward, not a shop purchase; maximum XYZZY hardware includes it.

Mission flags retain the original TP low-nibble states and default to unstarted in older saves. [Source profile and fidelity boundaries](docs/BBC-EXECUTIVE-MISSIONS.md) records the selected revision, source quirks, tests and the remaining differences in the native simulation and briefing presentation.

## XYZZY

Type `xyzzy` in the game to become Commander Clarke and open the secret menu. Your Cobra Mk III receives every equipment upgrade, a full missile rack and fuel, and restored shields and energy. Existing cargo, cash, location and progress are retained. Press Return or Escape to return to the previous screen with those upgrades. A new game starts as Jameson again.

| Key | Option |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Cycle white wireframe → coloured wireframe → shaded panels → white wireframe |
| 1 | Fit docking computers |
| 2 | Fit maximum hardware and restore shields, energy, fuel and missile rack |
| 3 | Unlimited missiles |
| 4 | Unlimited fuel |
| 5 | Unlimited hyperspace range, with no jump fuel cost |
| 6 | Auto-trade |
| 7 | Optional speech using the Mac's synthesiser |
| 8 | Unlimited cargo capacity |
| 9 | Unlimited cash: purchases do not debit the balance |

White wireframes are the default. Coloured wireframes identify hulls by colour; shaded panels draw opaque faces with flat directional lighting. The chosen display is saved with the commander. Old saves without a display choice load with white wireframes. Unlimited fuel and hyperspace distance are controlled here with 4 and 5; the paused-game @ shortcut has been removed.

Press K, use Command-K, or choose **Help → Player Key Guide** for the controls relevant to your current activity. The guide covers flight, station screens, trading, equipment, charts, pause, the title/demo and XYZZY. Arrow keys change pages; K, Return or Escape closes it. The simulation pauses while the guide is open and resumes on the same screen.

Auto-trade runs when enabled while docked and on subsequent docking. It sells the hold, refuels, and buys profitable exports for the selected destination. If the target is the current system, it chooses a reachable route using profit, distance and government risk. Pricing follows the original economy calculation; it evaluates all possible destination market fluctuations and buys only goods with a positive conservative margin. Slaves, narcotics and firearms are included when profitable, and illegal cargo affects legal status on launch. Cash, local stock and hold capacity constrain purchases unless the corresponding secret option is enabled. Unlimited cargo cannot be disabled while the hold exceeds the normal bay capacity; sell excess cargo first.

## Architecture

`EliteCore` contains the deterministic simulation, original galaxy and commodity calculations, commander persistence, raster graphics and sound synthesis. `GameRuntime` is a Swift actor owning the game and controls. The Mac app sends commands to that actor and draws immutable snapshots on the main actor. Ships and markets are value types within the simulation actor, avoiding a task per object and preserving update order. The window loop is cancelled on close; losing focus clears held controls.

`EliteMac` is the native AppKit window, keyboard adapter, drawing and audio/speech output. Rendering preserves the 256 by 256 logical layout while drawing ships, planets, stars, charts and cockpit shapes directly at the window's native resolution, including Retina displays and window resizing. Curves and lines are antialiased; the original bitmap font remains sharp with nearest-neighbour scaling. Persistent stars move through three-dimensional space with the ship's speed, pitch and roll, using subpixel positions and small round points without diagonal tails. Changing throttle cannot teleport the starfield. Shaded faces clip against the near plane and viewport and draw from back to front. Display preferences, star motion and contextual help remain value state owned by the game actor. Speech and sound are independent native reproductions, not recordings or ROM playback.

## Validation and SonarQube

```sh
make coverage-check
make threads
make address
make leaks
make ci
make readme-media
make sonar
```

Coverage is measured across every Swift source file, including the Mac adapter, with an 80% minimum and no source exclusions. Tests cover original Lave/Zaonce/Diso galaxy fixtures, market invariants, economy-based and illegal auto-trading, unlimited resources, combat, navigation, native key translation, frame conversion, actor concurrency and commander save validation. Wireframe regressions check flat, outward-facing hull surfaces, every projected silhouette segment across 7,200 hull orientations, and unclipped title-screen rotations. `make threads` runs Thread Sanitizer; `make address` runs Address Sanitizer; `make leaks` runs a repeated actor, mission and native frame lifecycle probe under macOS `leaks`, saving evidence in `reports/leaks.txt`. These tools detect problems in exercised paths and do not prove the absence of defects in every possible session.

GitHub CI runs on pull requests and pushes to `main`. Pull requests run coverage, sanitizers and leak checks without the Sonar token. SonarQube analysis runs only on committed `main`, verifies the remote main branch and Previous version new-code definition, then uses the exact 40-character commit SHA as `sonar.projectVersion`. Analysis fails if the token or required settings are missing. Configure `SONAR_TOKEN` locally; the repository's Actions secret is already configured. Badges above link to the project's SonarQube metrics.

## Public repository protections

`main` requires a pull request, an up-to-date branch and the successful `quality` check from GitHub Actions. The rules apply to administrators, require resolved review conversations and linear history, and block force pushes and branch deletion. Stephen is the only account with write access. Approving reviews are optional while there is only one maintainer; a second maintainer can be added before requiring independent approval.

Actions use read-only repository tokens, cannot approve pull requests, and run only the two approved GitHub actions pinned to full commit SHAs. Every external contributor's workflow run needs approval. Secret scanning, push protection, dependency alerts and security updates are enabled. [Security reporting](SECURITY.md) explains how to report a vulnerability privately.

The SonarQube project is public so its metrics and badges are readable. Analysis and project administration remain restricted to the Owners group; anonymous visitors have no write permissions.

Automatic analysis is disabled, including on the legacy duplicate `stephenlclarke_elite2` project. The authoritative project is `stephenlclarke_elite`, linked by the badges above; its main-only scanner verifies this setting before analysis.

The README screenshot and animated tape are generated from deterministic scenes using the same drawing commands as the live game, following the Pacman and Defender README media convention. Regenerate both with `make readme-media`.

## Fidelity and references

The [cross-version research reference](docs/research/README.md) contains separate deduplicated mission, ship and alien catalogues, version mappings, source provenance and mission replication specifications. It distinguishes documented historical behaviour from implementation recommendations and explicitly lists unresolved fidelity requirements.

This is an independent implementation, and exact historical parity has not yet been established. The procedural galaxy and economy have reference fixtures, and the two Executive naval mission rules follow the pinned Second Processor source. Most ship meshes, raster font, physics, AI, docking sequence, demo text and audio remain independent approximations. All original ship blueprints, original goat-soup descriptions, witchspace encounters and binary commander-file interoperability remain outside the currently implemented parity boundary. See [COMPATIBILITY.md](COMPATIBILITY.md) for the explicit comparison checklist.

The primary reference is [Mark Moxon's Executive version analysis](https://elite.bbcelite.com/deep_dives/secrets_of_the_executive_version.html), with [the 6502 Second Processor variant comparison](https://elite.bbcelite.com/6502sp/releases.html) and [the BBC Micro disc source documentation](https://elite.bbcelite.com/disc/). Original Elite was created by Ian Bell and David Braben. Numerical mission ship blueprint data is attributed in the mission source profile. This repository does not bundle their assembly source, binaries, ROMs or Mark Moxon's commentary. Mission briefing prose is independently worded.
