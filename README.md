A native Swift macOS space trading game presented by XYZZY as the Right-On Commander version. Its historical reference is the BBC Micro 6502 Second Processor Executive version of Elite. It uses keyboard flight controls, white wireframe ships by default, a colour cockpit scanner, procedural galaxies, and the original-style bitmap font requested for this remake. Normal games begin as Commander Jameson with a standard Cobra Mk III and 100 credits. Entering XYZZY switches to Commander Clarke with maximum hardware, 1,000,000 starting credits and shaded panels; white and coloured wireframes remain selectable.

## Build and run

Requires macOS 14 or newer and Swift 6.0 or newer (Xcode or the Command Line Tools).

```sh
git clone git@github.com:stephenlclarke/elite.git
cd elite
make run
```

The Run action builds and opens `dist/Elite.app`. You can also open that app from Finder after building it. The project has no external game assets or package dependencies. It starts with an automatic XYZZY demo, then a rotating Right-On Commander title screen. Rotating startup ships use shaded panels independently of your saved flight display preference. Press Space to enter the commander screen and 0 to launch.

### Trackpad flight

Place the pointer over the game before touching the trackpad. Touch gently; no click or repeated swipe is needed. Controls repeat on every game update while your fingers remain on the pad.

| Fingers | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Two | Steer without changing speed: up/down pitches, left/right rolls |
| One | Slow down along the current heading, stopping at zero speed |
| Three | Accelerate along the current heading, stopping at maximum speed |
| None | Centre the yoke, stop pitch/roll and cruise at the current speed |

Two-finger steering uses the midpoint of your fingers relative to the centre of the physical trackpad. Hold off centre to keep turning; return to the centre to stop turning and cruise. A small central dead zone prevents drift. Changing finger count switches modes immediately, so dropping from three fingers to two holds the speed you have reached. One- and three-finger speed changes keep the yoke centred.

Trackpad flight operates in cockpit views with manual control. Pause, help, docking, the docking computer, other screens, lost focus and cancelled gestures clear its input. Keyboard flight remains available. macOS Mission Control and three-finger dragging may intercept touches; assign system gestures to four fingers or disable three-finger dragging if three-finger acceleration does not register. Physical three-finger delivery depends on those system settings.

### Spin Hold prototype

Press V, or choose **Game → Toggle Spin Hold**, to enter the explicit spin mode. Draw a deliberate one-finger circle around the physical trackpad's centre to select clockwise or anticlockwise roll. Every recognised circle commands a gentle 12 degrees/second, independently of how quickly you draw it. CW and CCW are defined from the pilot's forward cockpit view. Pure roll preserves the forward heading.

| Fingers, in Spin Hold | Action |
| --- | --- |
| One, held still | Gradually reduce roll-speed magnitude to zero, without reversing |
| Two | Hold roll speed; move together to aim using pitch and yaw |
| Three | Gradually increase roll-speed magnitude to the cap |
| None | Keep the set spin speed, allowing your hand to lift and reposition |

The HUD shows Spin Hold state, CW/CCW direction, an arrow and actual degrees/second. It shows the target separately while the ship is changing rate, plus circle-recognition and speed-adjustment feedback. Press B, or choose **Game → Stop Spin**, to command a smooth stop and return to normal controls. V also exits smoothly. An opposite circle commands the other direction through a gradual stop and reversal. Help, pause, other screens, focus loss, cancelled gestures, docking and the docking computer cancel spin immediately. The read-only Mission Gallery freezes and restores Spin Hold and AUTO instead.

Keyboard alternatives are [ for anticlockwise, ] for clockwise, and held - / = or + to slow / speed up. In Spin Hold, Left/Right provide yaw and X/S provide pitch. Space and slash still change forward cruising speed. Normal-flight trackpad gestures retain the separate forward-speed mapping above; Spin Hold fingers control roll speed instead.

Prototype defaults are 12 degrees/second initially, 6 degrees/second of rate adjustment per second, a 60 degrees/second cap and a 24 degrees/second-per-second response limit. Contact counts must remain stable for 120 ms before changing rate. Circles need at least 300 degrees of consistent motion, 16 samples and 85% direction consistency, at a normalized radius of 0.12-0.46 around the pad centre; jumps over 45 degrees between samples are rejected. Two-finger aiming starts from a fresh reference whenever the count changes, with a 2% dead zone and full deflection after 25% of pad travel. These are tunable prototype values, not findings from player testing. Native touch hardware and macOS gesture interaction still require an on-device check; no global gesture settings are changed by the game.

Commander saves, including secret options, live at `~/.xyzzy/elite/commander.json`. Saving is atomic; loading validates the entire commander before replacing actor state.

## Original BBC naval missions

The original two BBC Second Processor missions are enabled, including the Executive build. They begin automatically when you dock and qualify; there is no acceptance menu or deadline. An incoming message appears for two seconds before the briefing. Space, Return or Escape continues through the pages. K opens the relevant key guide; Command-P pauses the briefing or game.

The Constrictor Hunt starts in galaxy 1 or 2 at 256 combat points. Read system data with 6 for the original clue trail; clue visits are optional. The stolen ship appears at Orarra in galaxy 2. It has 252 energy, ECM, four missiles and bomb immunity. Ordinary lasers cannot damage it; a military laser inflicts five damage per hit, a quarter of its normal 23 points rounded down. Returning to a station after its removal awards 5,000 credits and 256 combat points once. The Executive source also treats the target leaving the local space bubble as mission completion; this historical quirk is retained.

Thargoid Plans starts on docking in galaxy 3 after the Hunt is finished and your tally reaches 1,280. Report to Ceerdi, collect the encoded plans, then dock at Birera. Visiting Birera before collection does nothing. The plans occupy no cargo space and survive commander save/load. Carrying them enables the original 56-in-256 Thargoid ambush predicate at eligible spawning checks, with a mothership and Thargon companion. Motherships can release six additional drones when damaged; surviving drones stop fighting after the last mothership is destroyed. Delivery replaces a standard energy unit with the naval unit and adds 256 combat points once, with no cash payment. Energy recharge follows the original 1:2:3 ratio for an unupgraded, standard and naval unit. The naval unit is a mission reward, not a shop purchase; maximum XYZZY hardware includes it.

Mission flags retain the original TP low-nibble states and default to unstarted in older saves. [Source profile and fidelity boundaries](docs/BBC-EXECUTIVE-MISSIONS.md) records the selected revision, source quirks, tests and the remaining differences in the native simulation and briefing presentation.

## XYZZY

Type `xyzzy` in the game to become Commander Clarke and open the secret menu. On first activation you receive 1,000,000 CR, with finite cash accounting, maximum supported hardware, four additional fuel units (35 LY total capacity), a Military navigation computer, a full missile rack, fuel, shields and energy. The 21-LY Military jump cap and remaining fuel still limit each jump. Unlimited missiles, fuel replenishment, cargo, auto-trade, speech, AUTO targeting, automatic ECM and Military retaliation start enabled; shaded panels are selected. Naval lasers, Capital Ship shields, Military targeting and Naval Energy Unit are fitted, while Mining capability, ECM and scoops are retained.

Auto-trade immediately sells and restocks when activation happens while docked, so the initial grant can be spent immediately. Cash then changes through ordinary purchases, sales, fuel and rewards. Reopening XYZZY or loading a save never replenishes cash; re-entry retains switch choices and refits maximum hardware. Old XYZZY saves with unlimited cash receive the finite starting balance once, then persist the migration; finite old balances and ordinary commanders are preserved. Old unlimited-jump flags cannot bypass current jump limits. Location and mission progress remain intact. A new game starts as Jameson again.

| --- | --- |
| 0 | Cycle white wireframe → coloured wireframe → shaded panels → white wireframe |
| 1 | Fit at least the Docking targeting tier, preserving any higher tier |
| 2 | Fit maximum hardware and restore shields, energy, fuel and missile rack |
| 3 | Unlimited missiles |
| 4 | Unlimited fuel |
| 5 | Show current fuel/nav jump limit; no unlimited-distance override |
| 6 | Auto-trade |
| 7 | Optional speech using the Mac's synthesiser |
| 8 | Unlimited cargo capacity |
| 9 | Show actual cash balance; first XYZZY activation starts with 1,000,000 CR |
| A | AUTO target assist |
| B | Military ECM-confirmed return fire |
| E | Automatic ECM |

Normal games default to white wireframes; XYZZY activation defaults to shaded panels. Coloured wireframes identify hulls by colour; shaded panels draw opaque faces with flat directional lighting. The chosen display is saved with the commander. Old saves without a display choice load with white wireframes. Key 4 controls fuel replenishment. Jump distance always obeys fuel cells, current fuel and the navigation computer; the paused-game @ shortcut has been removed.

Press K, use Command-K, or choose **Help → Player Key Guide** for the controls relevant to your current activity. The guide covers flight, station screens, trading, equipment, charts, pause, the title/demo and XYZZY. Arrow keys change pages; K, Return or Escape closes it. The simulation pauses while the guide is open and resumes on the same screen.

Auto-trade runs when enabled while docked and on subsequent docking. It sells the hold, refuels, and buys profitable exports for the selected destination. If the target is the current system, it chooses a reachable route using profit, distance and government risk. Pricing follows the original economy calculation; it evaluates all possible destination market fluctuations and buys only goods with a positive conservative margin. Slaves, narcotics and firearms are included when profitable, and illegal cargo affects legal status on launch. Cash and local stock always constrain XYZZY purchases; hold capacity is unlimited while its switch is on. Auto-trade pays for a full tank before buying exports when fuel replenishment is off; insufficient cash stops export purchases with an explanation. Unlimited cargo cannot be disabled while the hold exceeds the normal bay capacity; sell excess cargo first.

## Cockpit and AUTO assistance

The flight view uses the full window width and native resolution, with antialiased wireframes and small point stars. Resize the window or enter full screen using the green window button. The original bitmap font stays at a compact readable size. Instruments are labelled Front shield, Rear shield, Fuel, Cabin temp, Laser heat, Altitude, Speed, Roll, Pitch and Energy; signed controls have a centre marker and neutral label. Status names are explicit. The decorative planet has been removed. The central scanner expands with the window, while supporting bars have a maximum width so they stay compact in full screen.

The missile rack shows one icon per finite missile, `None` for an empty rack, or one icon with the literal `~` for unlimited missiles. Armed and Unarmed are written as well as coloured.

With a fitted targeting computer, press **R** repeatedly to select eligible ships. All ships actually visible in the front renderer come first, nearest first, followed by offscreen ships nearest first if your tier supports them. IDs break distance ties. Stations follow the ship groups at Docking tier and above. Cargo canisters, asteroids and missiles are excluded. Front visibility uses the same mesh projection and clipping as rendering even from a rear or side view, including partly visible hulls. A cycle keeps its order while ships move; membership, equipment or viewport changes reconcile unvisited targets, and wrapping starts a fresh snapshot. A cyan lock marker and offscreen cue identify the target. AUTO faces and tracks ordinary ships at unchanged cruising speed, with Spin Hold available. **Q** or manual steering disengages.

Station AUTO completes docking without further input. It routes outside a 400-unit safety sphere, settles on the entrance axis at 1,000 units, matches facing, rotational phase and rate, then controls acceleration and braking during final approach. It holds or retreats if alignment is inadequate and enters through the rendered doorway using the simulation's docking and collision checks. The HUD and voice announce Approaching, Aligning, Matching spin, Final approach and Docked. Q, manual controls, Spin Hold, pause or focus loss cancels; controlled forward cruise is retained on manual cancellation. C also engages station guidance through the same targeting-tier capability. The old standalone Docking Computers item has been removed; older saves migrate to at least Docking tier without downgrading a higher tier. These native guidance thresholds are prototype choices. Tests cover front/behind, tilted/off-axis and away-facing starts, both spin directions and high initial speed; starting inside station geometry is not a supported safe starting condition.

## Targeting computers and target information

Upgrades are cumulative, with one effective installed tier. Prices are in credits; Basic and Military use twice their matching laser prices, with graduated intermediate prices following the requested laser-related scale. Equipment rows use a compact font and a right-aligned credit column inside a centred table capped at 680 points, keeping names and prices close in full screen.

| Computer | Price (CR) | Added capability |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Basic | 800 | Acquire front-visible ships and face the selected ship |
| Advanced | 2,000 | Also acquire known offscreen ships |
| Docking | 4,000 | Also target stations and complete automatic docking |
| Identification | 6,000 | Live zoomed target camera, model and status |
| Pirate | 10,000 | Cargo, origin and destination information |
| Military | 12,000 | Shields, laser heat, fuel, missiles, ECM and ECM-confirmed retaliation |

The upper-right target panel is expanded by default in full screen and compact in a window; I changes it. Its live ship rendering copies the actual target pose without moving the flight camera. Lower tiers receive only a minimal target identifier. Ship missile counts and ECM equipment/activity come from simulation state. NPC cargo, routes, shield banks, fuel and laser heat are not currently modelled, so the panel explicitly shows Unknown or Unavailable rather than player readings or invented data. Stations show entrance, rotation and range information instead of ship armament.

Military identifies the actual craft that launched a player-targeted missile and establishes a weapons lock without taking over steering or Spin Hold. After that missile is confirmed destroyed by the player's ECM, it can fire one retaliatory missile per attacker per activation. It waits for the ECM field and the one-second missile cooldown, consumes normal ammunition unless unlimited, and requires a living source and forward missile lock. Missing ammunition, invalid source/lock or disabled equipment cannot produce a success announcement. Pending return fire expires after three game seconds. U clears pending return fire and disarms; XYZZY B toggles it. Automatic ECM still requires its existing XYZZY switch and fitted equipment; manual ECM can also confirm retaliation.

## Trading colours

Buy Cargo, Sell Cargo and Market Prices use consistent category colours: green Agricultural, cyan Consumer, yellow Industrial, blue Materials and orange Restricted cargo. A labelled legend explains the colours. Slaves, narcotics and firearms are the restricted goods in the implemented launch-legal-status rules. Category colours do not predict profit or imply that other goods are buyable. Separate Action text states BUY, SELL, NO STOCK, NO CASH, HOLD FULL, NONE HELD or DOCK TO TRADE, alongside price, stock and hold counts. Arrows or scrolling browse every commodity; category colours also appear in Inventory.

## Navigation, fuel storage and charts

Equip Ship groups every supported item under Fuel & Storage, Lasers, Shield Boosters, Targeting Computers, Navigation Computers, Energy Systems, Defence & Ordnance, Cargo & Collection, Travel and Emergency Equipment. Yellow headings cannot be selected or bought. Item names are indented, prices/statuses are right aligned, arrows skip headings, and clicking or trackpad scrolling browses the list. Unavailable items show their required tech level; Naval/Elite grants remain visible without a fictitious purchase price. Selection follows item identity when sections reorder the display.

Each Fuel Storage Unit costs **2,000 CR** and adds 7 LY of capacity. Up to four additional units fit: total capacity is 7, 14, 21, 28 or 35 LY. The row shows the fitted count and maximum; fitting an empty unit never grants free fuel. Shop and auto-trade refuelling use the installed capacity. Saves without a unit count retain the original 7-LY tank.

| Navigation computer | Price (CR) | Single-jump cap | Destination search range |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| None | Included | 7 LY | Route planning unavailable |
| Basic | 1,000 | 7 LY | Installed fuel capacity + 7 LY |
| Advanced | 3,000 | 14 LY | Installed fuel capacity + 14 LY |
| Military | 9,000 | 21 LY | Entire current galaxy |

Actual reach is the lesser of the computer cap and current remaining fuel; fuel capacity cannot bypass the cap. Search uses installed capacity, so an empty tank does not shrink search range. This applies in XYZZY too: its 35-LY tank and Military computer permit at most 21 LY per jump. Normal jumps debit distance-based fuel; the optional XYZZY fuel switch replenishes the tank during updates. Original distances are quantised in 0.4-LY steps.

On either chart, M cycles Economy, Government and Technology colour modes with a labelled red-to-green legend. Short-range planets are filled discs sized from their radius; the current system has a cyan ring and the selected system a white ring and crosshair. Click to select. Slash opens the galactic chart's searchable list of all 256 planets, with case-insensitive filtering, arrows, Return, Escape, scrolling and Clear. Chart search consumes text input without firing weapons or steering.

The short-range chart distinguishes dotted green search range, dashed yellow maximum equipment reach and solid cyan fuel-limited reach. When fuel and maximum reach coincide, the legend states that explicitly. Military search is labelled as the entire galaxy, with Z cycling maximum-jump, search-fit and actual-fuel zoom; offscreen boundaries remain described in the legend.

Press N on a chart to compare Fewest Jumps, Safest and Trade Opportunities routes. A Best Cargo Sale detour is offered when its estimated return exceeds direct destination sales. Identical paths combine labels; all paths use legal hops, current fuel for the first leg, and station refuelling to installed capacity thereafter. Risk reflects government, and trade/cargo figures are conservative market estimates rather than guaranteed profits or guaranteed affordable refuelling. An empty tank must be refuelled before planning.

Return sets a route without launching, jumping or docking. Dock at each stop; launching preloads the next system and announces it, and H starts each jump manually. N inspects the stored route. C clears it in the route panel; a manual chart destination, off-route arrival or galactic jump clears it too. Route progress survives save/load, and auto-trade evaluates the next planned leg without replacing it. Route browsing pauses flight.

## Shields, Naval lasers and voice

Each standard booster multiplies base front and rear capacity by `1.5 × (matching laser damage / Pulse laser damage)`. Mining damage is 50, Pulse and Beam are 15, and Military is 23, so Mining provides more capacity than Military. Shop order remains Pulse, Mining, Beam, Military, Capital Ship. Purchases that would reduce capacity are blocked explicitly. Capital is three times Military capacity. Boosters do not stack; recharge remains one shield unit per second, with energy-unit regeneration independent.

| Booster | Price (CR) | Capacity versus base | Each shield bank |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Standard | Included | 1 | 255 |
| Pulse | 300 | 1.5 | 382.5 |
| Mining | 600 | 5 | 1,275 |
| Beam | 750 | 1.5 | 382.5 |
| Military | 4,500 | 2.3 | 586.5 |
| Capital Ship | Elite reward / XYZZY | 6.9 | 1,759.5 |

Standard booster prices are exactly 75% of the corresponding laser price and use that laser's technology requirement. Capital is awarded once when the authoritative combat rank reaches Elite, including migration of already-Elite saves. The earned flag is saved; XYZZY grants the hardware without granting rank or claiming an earned reward. Shields, HUD normalisation, damage, docking resets and save migration use the effective capacity.

The Cobra Mk III has independent Front, Left, Right and Rear laser mounts. A new Jameson has a front Pulse Laser and three empty mounts. Equip Ship always shows all four fittings; Left/Right or clicking a mount chooses where the selected laser is installed. Return purchases one installation at its listed price and replaces only that mount. Fitted status and confirmations name the chosen destination. Each cockpit view fires its own mounted laser, with its own damage and firing interval; an empty mount cannot fire. Heat and energy remain shared ship resources. Commander Status and Inventory list all four mounts, and arrows, scrolling or Fn-Up/Fn-Down expose every equipment row. Legacy global laser saves resolve to one effective front laser, preserving other equipment without inventing paid side lasers. XYZZY grants Naval lasers on all four mounts, including migration of old XYZZY saves, and retains Mining as specialised equipment. Naval damage is a chosen native default of 64 per hit, or 16 against the Constrictor's quarter-damage shield; heat/energy cost and rapid firing use the existing laser mechanics. Naval lasers and Capital shields are grant/reward equipment, with no invented shop price or availability.

A locked target destroyed by the player displays and voices **Target destroyed - your kill** once. Actual player laser, missile and energy-bomb damage carries kill attribution; unrelated destruction, range loss or despawn announces Target lost without awarding a player kill. Manual switching does not announce a loss, and rank/bounty feedback remains separate.

Every gameplay notification uses a shared event queue for its on-screen text and voice, including combat congratulations, warnings, navigation, docking, equipment, rewards and naval-message events. Combat rank promotion emits Right on, Commander. Repeated snapshots never repeat speech; urgent warnings are prioritised and backlog is coalesced. Missile identification, ECM confirmation and actual return-fire launches use the same queue without duplicate generic announcements. Speech preferences remain respected: XYZZY 7 or paused colon toggles voice. Continuous HUD readings and entire menu screens are not spoken automatically.

Automatic ECM requires active XYZZY mode, its E switch, fitted ECM and enough energy. It respects the 32-update field cooldown. Speech announces **Missile Detected**, **ECM Activated**, then the actual count of missiles destroyed by that activation, with singular/plural wording. It never announces a successful zero count or credits enemy ECM. Repeated speech waves are queued and coalesced. Secret key 7 controls speech; flight key E still activates ECM manually. Fuel Scoops are fitted by default and can be refitted with secret F. Pass a cargo canister below the forward nose within 85 native units to collect it; a full finite hold leaves it in space. Inactive Thargons can be scooped as Alien Items. Native canisters currently contain one Food unit; solar fuel collection is not implemented. Unlimited fuel remains independently controlled by 4.

## Mission Gallery

Press **L** in space or at a station to inspect all currently implemented missions, including locked and completed ones. Wide windows show a mission list and detail side by side; narrow windows open a detail page with Escape to go back. Use arrows and Return, click a mission, and scroll with the trackpad or **Fn-Up/Fn-Down** on a MacBook. L closes directly.

The gallery shows Active, Available and Complete counts, prerequisites, briefing, destination, rewards, next step and a chronological timeline with Complete, Current, Upcoming or Locked states. Each BBC mission has three required milestones. Unlock scores and optional clues are separate; no travel percentage or invented historical events are shown. Destination spoilers are labelled.

Browsing is read-only. Flight time, movement, combat, AUTO and Spin Hold freeze while the gallery is open. Closing restores the exact prior screen and paused state and clears held weapon, keyboard and touch input. A pending naval briefing must be completed before opening the gallery. Older commander saves remain compatible.

## Architecture

`EliteCore` contains the deterministic simulation, original galaxy and commodity calculations, commander persistence, raster graphics and sound synthesis. `GameRuntime` is a Swift actor owning the game and controls. The Mac app sends commands to that actor and draws immutable snapshots on the main actor. Ships and markets are value types within the simulation actor, avoiding a task per object and preserving update order. The window loop is cancelled on close; losing focus clears held controls.

`EliteMac` is the native AppKit window, keyboard adapter, drawing and audio/speech output. Flight rendering uses a responsive full-width native-resolution viewport and compact cockpit, including Retina displays and window resizing. Equipment, markets, commander status, inventory, charts, route planning and mission browsing use responsive native layouts; system data, demo, title and help retain their original logical layout. Curves and lines are antialiased; the original bitmap font remains sharp with nearest-neighbour scaling. Persistent stars move through three-dimensional space with the ship's speed, pitch and roll, using subpixel positions and small round points without diagonal tails. Changing throttle cannot teleport the starfield. Shaded faces clip against the near plane and viewport and draw from back to front. Display preferences, star motion and contextual help remain value state owned by the game actor. Speech and sound are independent native reproductions, not recordings or ROM playback.

## Validation and SonarQube

```sh
make coverage-check
make threads
make address
make leaks
make ci
make readme-media
make sonar
```

Coverage is measured across every Swift source file, including the Mac adapter, with an 80% minimum and no source exclusions. Tests cover original Lave/Zaonce/Diso galaxy fixtures, market invariants, economy-based and illegal auto-trading, resource limits, counted fuel storage, finite XYZZY cash, grouped equipment, route planning, combat, navigation, native key translation, frame conversion, actor concurrency and commander save validation. Wireframe regressions check flat, outward-facing hull surfaces, every projected silhouette segment across 7,200 hull orientations, and unclipped title-screen rotations. `make threads` runs Thread Sanitizer; `make address` runs Address Sanitizer; `make leaks` runs a repeated actor, mission and native frame lifecycle probe under macOS `leaks`, saving evidence in `reports/leaks.txt`. These tools detect problems in exercised paths and do not prove the absence of defects in every possible session.

GitHub CI runs on pull requests and pushes to `main`. Pull requests run coverage, sanitizers and leak checks without the Sonar token. SonarQube analysis runs only on committed `main`, verifies the remote main branch and Previous version new-code definition, then uses the exact 40-character commit SHA as `sonar.projectVersion`. Analysis fails if the token or required settings are missing. Configure `SONAR_TOKEN` locally; the repository's Actions secret is already configured. Badges above link to the project's SonarQube metrics.

## Public repository protections

`main` requires a pull request, an up-to-date branch and the successful `quality` check from GitHub Actions. The rules apply to administrators, require resolved review conversations and linear history, and block force pushes and branch deletion. Stephen is the only account with write access. Approving reviews are optional while there is only one maintainer; a second maintainer can be added before requiring independent approval.

Actions use read-only repository tokens, cannot approve pull requests, and run only the two approved GitHub actions pinned to full commit SHAs. Every external contributor's workflow run needs approval. Secret scanning, push protection, dependency alerts and security updates are enabled. [Security reporting](SECURITY.md) explains how to report a vulnerability privately.

The SonarQube project is public so its metrics and badges are readable. Analysis and project administration remain restricted to the Owners group; anonymous visitors have no write permissions.

Automatic analysis is disabled, including on the legacy duplicate `stephenlclarke_elite2` project. The authoritative project is `stephenlclarke_elite`, linked by the badges above; its main-only scanner verifies this setting before analysis.

The README screenshot and animated tape are generated from deterministic scenes using the same drawing commands as the live game, following the Pacman and Defender README media convention. Regenerate both with `make readme-media`.

## Fidelity and references

The independently maintained local `docs/research/` reference contains separate deduplicated mission, ship and alien catalogues, version mappings, source provenance and mission replication specifications. It distinguishes documented historical behaviour from implementation recommendations and explicitly lists unresolved fidelity requirements.

This is an independent implementation, and exact historical parity has not yet been established. The procedural galaxy and economy have reference fixtures, and the two Executive naval mission rules follow the pinned Second Processor source. Most ship meshes, raster font, physics, AI, docking sequence, demo text and audio remain independent approximations. All original ship blueprints, original goat-soup descriptions, witchspace encounters and binary commander-file interoperability remain outside the currently implemented parity boundary. See [COMPATIBILITY.md](COMPATIBILITY.md) for the explicit comparison checklist.

The primary reference is [Mark Moxon's Executive version analysis](https://elite.bbcelite.com/deep_dives/secrets_of_the_executive_version.html), with [the 6502 Second Processor variant comparison](https://elite.bbcelite.com/6502sp/releases.html) and [the BBC Micro disc source documentation](https://elite.bbcelite.com/disc/). Original Elite was created by Ian Bell and David Braben. Numerical mission ship blueprint data is attributed in the mission source profile. This repository does not bundle their assembly source, binaries, ROMs or Mark Moxon's commentary. Mission briefing prose is independently worded.

All ship data, including Frontier capabilities and shipyard refit research, is retained; see [ship data scope](docs/SHIP-DATA-SCOPE.md).
