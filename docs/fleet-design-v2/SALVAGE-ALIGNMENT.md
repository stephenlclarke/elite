2. Overcome its real shields using lasers, missiles, EMP or a combination. Laser/missile preparation is optional.
3. Cause an actual commander-attributed EMP electrical disable.
4. Establish lawful salvage authority, actual occupant clearance and protection of mission originals.
5. Explicitly activate a fitted eligible Salvage Beam on the same target.

## EMP upgrades and ship capabilities

EMP applies a hit to the impacted shield bank first, then unused pulse power to electrical reserve. It does not directly damage hull energy. A sufficiently strong hit can disable a small craft without preparatory laser/missile damage; larger/tougher craft may require multiple hits, with intervening real recovery and ECM still relevant.

The physical EMP capacitor is separate from hull survival energy and the main electrical supply. Paid upgrade axes are pulse power, stored-shot capacity and recharge rate. Preserve module/bank identity, finite remaining charge, elapsed recharge and real main-system transfer costs across menus, pauses, saves, refits and hull exchanges. Multiple stored shots do not bypass the existing ten-second trigger warmup or 1.5-second release cooldown.

The researcher's suggested power multipliers, capacities, recharge values, transfer efficiency and beam grades are explicitly proposed balance numbers. This fleet pack does not quietly approve new shop prices, technology requirements or predecessor bindings. A's equipment adapter consumes the existing workstream's eventual reviewed definitions. F shows actual pulse power, bank/available shots, refill time, release conditions and electrical draw. A large `energyCapacity` in a hull TOML is not a free fully upgraded EMP bank.

## Salvage Beam fitting and power

The beam is a separate branch under the Targeting Computer's Salvage Module. It requires the eligible installed core, Salvage Module, compatible emitter, cutter/salvage laser and Fuel Scoops. Ordinary targeting is not authority to cut. A valid explicit beam activation automatically starts the cutter/scoops only after safe close approach; it requires no second fire input. W/manual takeover or Q/cancel immediately stops automated cutting and applies the source's interruption rules.

For this fleet's new physical mount layout, the emitter uses one utility mount in addition to the cutter and scoop mounts. A capture kit therefore needs three mounts. Its emitter fitting allowance uses the workstream's proposed one/two/three equipment units by grade; the proposal validator budgets the strongest three-unit case. This is a new fleet fitting proposal, not a claim about an already released shop. A low-mount interceptor may need another owned specialist hull or an authorised loan to perform capture; it does not gain an invisible emitter because its target has valuable blueprints.

Required beam power increases with the target's calibrated volume, carried mass and emitter-to-nearest-solid-surface distance. Reach and power are separate continuous limits. The operator's actual force/reaction/compensation capability can impose a stricter limit. The beam cannot teleport, shrink or pull a hull through a station/ship/rock. Calibrated dimensions and physical force contracts are prerequisites; neither a normalised preview nor a new layout estimate supplies a measured beam rating. Show Unknown with the actual reason until those inputs exist.

Meter suppression only after a genuine EMP disable and a qualified powered beam lock. Losing lock, power, title, occupancy clearance, line of sight, heat margin or compatible intake stops work. Preserve the remaining recovery interval; do not restart a thirty-second timeout or regenerate sections/people on reload. Friendly pre-disabled mission sources keep their original consent/recovery rules and need no hostile attack.
